# The $275 Billion Heist — Outline Pressure Test

Structural review against the locked thesis. Flags only. No chapter rewrites.

## Method

Each chapter is tested on three criteria:

1. **Proof** — does the one-sentence claim advance the thesis (positioning over performance, written into law, protected by lobbying, invisible to funders)
2. **Length** — is the assigned page count proportional to the load the chapter carries
3. **Sequence** — does the reader arrive with exactly what they need, and nothing they do not

A chapter passes only if all three hold. Partial passes are flagged.

## Part V — The Outcome

### Ch 18 — The Flywheel (medium, 12-15)
- Proof: **flag**. By this point the reader already understands the loop from Ch 7, Ch 9, and Ch 10. A flywheel chapter at medium length risks restating what the reader just learned.
- Fix, option A: reduce to short (6-8) and let it function as a single clean diagram in prose.
- Fix, option B: keep medium length and give it the one dimension the earlier chapters do not supply — time. How the loop accelerates year over year, compounding through the data Dutch already has. If Ch 18 introduces acceleration, medium is justified. If it only names the loop, short is correct.

### Ch 19 — The Independent Physician (long, 20-25)
- Proof: passes. The human face of the thesis.
- Length: **flag**. At 20-25 pages the chapter invites voice drift. The style rules forbid reassurance, villainization, and argument ahead of evidence. Sustained human narrative at that length risks all three, because the emotional gravity of the material pulls the prose toward advocacy.
- Fix: reduce Ch 19 to medium (12-15). Expand Ch 20 (the disappearance numbers) from short to medium. The data should carry the human weight. This preserves the Part V payload and protects the voice.

### Ch 20 — The Disappearance (short, 6-8)
- Proof: passes.
- Length: see Ch 19 fix. If Ch 19 contracts, Ch 20 expands to medium.
- Sequence: passes.
