# Chapter 4. The Price Isn't the Price

Medicare is twelve payment systems.

Each has its own statute. Each has its own annual rulemaking cycle. Each has its own conversion factor or update mechanism. Each has its own eligibility rules for which entities can bill under it. Each sets its own rates for the same clinical work.

The system that applies to a given Medicare service is not chosen by the clinical content of the service. It is chosen by the CMS enrollment classification of the billing entity.

That is the mechanism Chapter 3 ended on.

This chapter names the twelve systems.

---

The first system is the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule.

The MPFS pays physicians and certain non-physician clinicians on a per-service basis. Each service is assigned three Relative Value Units: work, practice expense, and malpractice. The three RVUs sum to a total RVU. The total RVU is multiplied by an annually set conversion factor to produce the payment.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 414 subpart B.

The MPFS pays for the professional work of an office visit, an office procedure, a physician's interpretation of an imaging study, a surgeon's operative work across multiple settings. It does not pay for the facility in which the work happens.

That is the first system.

---

The second system is the Outpatient Prospective Payment System.

OPPS pays hospital outpatient departments on a per-encounter basis. Each service is grouped into an Ambulatory Payment Classification. Each APC has a scaled relative weight. The weight is multiplied by the OPPS conversion factor to produce the payment.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 419.

OPPS is the system that pays the hospital outpatient department for the facility side of a service. When a surgery happens at a hospital outpatient department, the hospital's OPPS facility payment sits on top of whatever MPFS professional payment the surgeon also receives. Two systems, one service.

That is the second system.

---

The third system is the Inpatient Prospective Payment System.

IPPS pays acute-care hospitals a single bundled payment per discharge. The discharge is coded into a Medicare Severity Diagnosis-Related Group. The MS-DRG carries a base payment that is then adjusted for the hospital's wage index, its disproportionate share status, and its graduate medical education activity.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 412.

IPPS is the system that pays for an inpatient admission. One payment, one discharge, many adjustments.

That is the third system.

---

The fourth system is the Ambulatory Surgical Center Payment System.

The ASC Payment System pays Medicare-certified ambulatory surgical centers on a per-procedure basis. The rates are set as a fraction of the OPPS APC for the same procedure. The fraction is updated annually by the ASC conversion factor, which runs below the OPPS conversion factor.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 416.

This is the system that produced half of the Chapter 3 ratios. Same procedure. Different conversion factor. Different rate.

That is the fourth system.

---

The fifth system is the Skilled Nursing Facility Prospective Payment System.

The SNF PPS pays Medicare-certified skilled nursing facilities a per-diem rate. The rate is adjusted for patient case mix under the Patient Driven Payment Model. The payment covers nursing, therapy, and non-therapy ancillary components.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 413 subpart J.

That is the fifth system.

---

The sixth system is the Home Health Prospective Payment System.

The HH PPS pays Medicare-certified home health agencies a case-mix adjusted payment per thirty-day period of care under the Patient-Driven Groupings Model.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 484 subpart E.

That is the sixth system.

---

The seventh system is the Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility Prospective Payment System.

The IRF PPS pays inpatient rehabilitation facilities and distinct-part rehabilitation units a per-discharge payment based on Case Mix Groups. To retain IRF classification, a facility is required to meet the 60 percent rule on qualifying patient conditions.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 412 subpart P.

The 60 percent rule is a classification test. The classification unlocks the payment system. The payment system unlocks a different rate than the inpatient hospital system does for similar work.

That is the seventh system.

---

The eighth system is the Long-Term Care Hospital Prospective Payment System.

The LTCH PPS pays long-term care hospitals a per-discharge rate using Medicare Severity Long-Term Care DRGs. A statutorily defined site-neutral rate applies to cases that do not meet the patient-criteria thresholds for the full LTCH rate.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 412 subpart O.

A site-neutral rate was written into the LTCH PPS by statute. Cases that do not meet the patient-criteria thresholds are paid at a rate equivalent to what they would receive under the inpatient hospital system. The statutory authors acknowledged, inside the LTCH system, that the same clinical content paid at different rates by different systems was a problem worth addressing inside that system.

The same principle has not been applied across the other eleven.

That is the eighth system.

---

The ninth system is the Inpatient Psychiatric Facility Prospective Payment System.

The IPF PPS pays freestanding psychiatric hospitals and distinct-part psychiatric units a per-diem rate. The rate is adjusted for patient characteristics, length of stay, and facility features.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 412 subpart N.

That is the ninth system.

---

The tenth system is the End-Stage Renal Disease Prospective Payment System.

The ESRD PPS pays Medicare-certified dialysis facilities a single bundled per-treatment rate. The bundle covers dialysis services, most injectable drugs, laboratory services, and supplies.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 413 subpart H.

That is the tenth system.

---

The eleventh system is the Medicare hospice benefit.

Medicare pays Medicare-certified hospices on a per-diem basis at four distinct levels of care: routine home care, continuous home care, inpatient respite care, and general inpatient care. The payments are subject to an aggregate annual cap per beneficiary.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 418 subpart G.

That is the eleventh system.

---

The twelfth system is the Critical Access Hospital payment methodology.

Critical Access Hospitals are paid 101 percent of reasonable cost. The payment applies to Medicare inpatient services, outpatient services, skilled nursing services, and laboratory services at the facility.

Critical Access Hospitals do not bill under the prospective payment systems at all. The payment method bypasses them.

The designation requires a rural location and a twenty-five-bed limit.

The regulatory anchor is 42 CFR 413.70 and 42 CFR 485 subpart F.

That is the twelfth system.

---

Twelve systems.

The Medicare Physician Fee Schedule. The Outpatient Prospective Payment System. The Inpatient Prospective Payment System. The Ambulatory Surgical Center Payment System. The Skilled Nursing Facility Prospective Payment System. The Home Health Prospective Payment System. The Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility Prospective Payment System. The Long-Term Care Hospital Prospective Payment System. The Inpatient Psychiatric Facility Prospective Payment System. The End-Stage Renal Disease Prospective Payment System. The hospice benefit. The Critical Access Hospital payment methodology.

Each of the twelve systems is governed by its own statutory authority. Its own annual rulemaking cycle. Its own conversion factor or update mechanism. Its own set of eligibility rules for which entities can bill under it.

Twelve systems. Twelve rate schedules. Twelve sets of rules for which room a service has to happen in to bill under which schedule.

---

A single clinical act can qualify for more than one of them.

A pembrolizumab infusion can be delivered in an office that bills under the MPFS. It can be delivered in a hospital outpatient department that bills under OPPS. It can be delivered in an inpatient setting that bills under IPPS.

The drug is the same. The pump is the same. The nurse is the same. The patient is the same.

The payment system that applies is decided elsewhere.

---

Where.

At the CMS enrollment classification of the billing entity, at the time of service.

When a physician practice enrolls as a CMS-855B group practice, the billing entity's services default to the MPFS. When a hospital enrolls a site as a hospital-based department under CMS-855A, the billing entity's services at that site default to OPPS. When an ambulatory surgical center completes the CMS-certification pathway under 42 CFR 416, its procedures default to the ASC Payment System.

The regulatory anchor for billing entity classification is 42 CFR 424 subpart P.

The classification is not chosen at the point of care. The classification is chosen upstream, on enrollment paperwork, sometimes years before the patient is scheduled.

The classification is the switch that routes the service into one of the twelve systems.

---

The classification is not a clinical datum.

Nothing in the CMS-855A, CMS-855B, CMS-855I, or CMS-855S enrollment forms captures a clinical judgment about a specific patient. The forms capture the identity of the billing entity. The type of ownership. The location. The services the entity is authorized to bill. Whether the entity has been certified, surveyed, and approved to enroll under a given payment system.

The payment system that applies to the pembrolizumab infusion is selected before the patient is referred. It is selected before the drug is ordered. It is selected before the infusion chair is pulled up. It is selected when the enrollment paperwork is filed and CMS issues its approval.

The clinical act conforms to the classification. The classification does not conform to the clinical act.

---

Twelve systems. Twelve rate schedules. One classification decision made upstream that picks which of the twelve applies.

That is the mechanism the rest of Part II documents.
