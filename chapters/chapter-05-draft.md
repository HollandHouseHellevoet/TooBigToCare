# Chapter 5. The Boxes

A physician orders a pembrolizumab infusion for a Medicare beneficiary.

The drug is Keytruda. The HCPCS code is J9271. The administration code is CPT 96365, intravenous infusion for therapy, prophylaxis, or diagnosis, initial, up to one hour. The dose is 200 milligrams, fixed, every three weeks.

The patient has a pembrolizumab-indicated malignancy. The dosing follows the FDA-approved prescribing information.

The clinical order is written. The infusion is scheduled.

From this point, the care is deterministic. The drug is the same drug. The nurse is the same nurse. The exam room is the same exam room. The pump is the same pump. The physician who signed the order is the same physician.

The payment is not deterministic. The payment depends on the building.

---

Two Place of Service codes can apply to the infusion.

Place of Service code 11 is office.

Place of Service code 22 is on-campus outpatient hospital.

The Place of Service code is entered on the claim form. It is a two-digit field. Nothing in the field describes the drug, the dose, the administration time, the nursing staff, or the physician. It describes the building.

Under POS 11, the claim routes to the Medicare Physician Fee Schedule.

Under POS 22, the claim routes to the Outpatient Prospective Payment System.

Same drug. Same dose. Same hour of infusion time. Two Place of Service codes. Two payment systems. Two different payments.

---

Consider how a site ends up with one Place of Service code rather than the other.

A hospital system acquires an independent oncology practice.

The acquisition does not change the clinical operations at the site. The exam rooms stay. The chairs stay. The pumps stay. The nursing staff stays. The physicians stay. The patient panel stays. The drugs continue to arrive from the same wholesaler. The appointment book continues to fill.

What changes is the billing entity.

Before the acquisition, the practice was enrolled with Medicare as a CMS-855B group practice. Services delivered at the practice billed under the MPFS at POS 11.

After the acquisition, the site is reassigned to the acquiring hospital's CMS-855A institutional enrollment. The CMS-855A submission names the site as a department of the hospital. It is submitted to the Medicare Administrative Contractor. It does not require any change in clinical staffing, protocols, or patient panel at the site.

The regulatory anchor for the reassignment is 42 CFR 424 subpart P. The enrollment application itself is the CMS Medicare Enrollment Application CMS-855A.

The same building continues to operate the same way. The billing entity is different.

---

A CMS-855A enrollment is not sufficient on its own to shift a site's services into OPPS.

For services performed at the acquired site to bill as hospital-based under OPPS, the hospital also documents compliance with 42 CFR 413.65. The regulation governs when a department qualifies as part of the main hospital for billing purposes. The applicable subsection is 42 CFR 413.65(d).

The subsection sets four criteria.

The first is common ownership. The acquired site is owned by the hospital.

The second is common supervision. The supervisory structure at the acquired site reports into the hospital's administrative structure.

The third is integrated clinical operations. The clinical operations at the acquired site are integrated with those of the main hospital.

The fourth is integrated financial records. The financial records of the acquired site are integrated with those of the main hospital.

The four criteria are documented in a compliance file. The compliance file is retained by the hospital. It is not a clinical record. No patient appears in it.

Once the CMS-855A enrollment reflects the acquired site, and once the 42 CFR 413.65(d) compliance file is on record, the hospital can submit claims for services performed at the site using Place of Service code 22 rather than Place of Service code 11.

The compliance file does not change what happens in the exam room. It changes what happens on the claim form.

---

At that point the payment split appears.

Under CY2026 Medicare rates, the service-level facility fee for CPT 96365 is $216.49 when billed at POS 22.

The rate is produced by OPPS APC 5693, Level 3 Drug Administration, at the CY2026 OPPS conversion factor of $91.415.

Under the 2025 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule, the same service-level fee for CPT 96365 is $57.90 when billed at POS 11. The rate is produced by 1.79 total Relative Value Units under the MPFS non-facility setting. CMS notes that these infusion administration codes are billable by the physician in the non-facility setting only.

$216.49 at POS 22. $57.90 at POS 11.

The same intravenous infusion. The same initial hour. The same drug. The same dose. The same physician. The same nurse. The same room.

Sourced: CMS CY2026 January OPPS Addendum B, APC 5693 on-campus rate at the CY2026 OPPS conversion factor of $91.415. ASH summary of CY2025 MPFS final rule.

A CY2026 policy note attaches. Excepted off-campus hospital outpatient departments billing the same code at POS 19 are paid at a PFS-equivalent rate of approximately $86.60 under a CY2026 drug administration site-neutral policy. The policy affects 61 HCPCS codes across APCs 5691 through 5694. The estimated CY2026 impact is approximately $290 million. The POS 22 on-campus path remains at full OPPS and is unaffected by the policy.

The site-neutral policy applies to the off-campus room. It does not apply to the on-campus room.

---

The drug layer sits on top of the administration layer.

Pembrolizumab (Keytruda, HCPCS J9271) was the single largest Medicare Part B drug by total spending in 2023, at $4.8 billion in allowed claims.

Source: MedPAC 2025 Data Book, Part B drug spending by HCPCS code.

J9271 is the highest-volume drug in the Medicare Part B program. It sits at the top of the Part B drug spending list. Whatever payment mechanism attaches to the drug is the mechanism that governs the largest single Part B drug exposure CMS has.

Under the April 2026 Medicare Part B Average Sales Price pricing file, a 200 milligram pembrolizumab dose is paid at $12,250.20. The payment methodology is ASP+6%. The payment limit is $61.251 per one milligram, multiplied by 200 milligrams. The ASP+6% payment is the same at POS 11 and POS 22.

The difference is on the acquisition side.

A non-340B site acquires the 200 milligram dose at approximately the Average Sales Price, approximately $11,557. The service-level margin on the drug for a non-340B site is the 6 percent add-on, approximately $693 before the administration fee.

A 340B covered entity acquires the same 200 milligram dose at a discount below ASP.

The exact 340B acquisition price for pembrolizumab is not publicly posted in an open-source table. HRSA ceiling prices are accessed through the 340B OPAIS Ceiling Price Lookup.

Two published federal discount anchors bound the estimate. The HHS Office of Inspector General, in a 2015 analysis of 340B ceiling prices, estimated the average 340B discount at 33.6 percent below ASP. CMS, in the CY2018 OPPS Final Rule in which it set 340B Part B drug reimbursement at ASP minus 22.5 percent, stated an estimate of the average 340B acquisition cost at 22.5 percent below ASP.

Applied to the 200 milligram pembrolizumab ASP, the two anchors place the 340B acquisition cost in a range of approximately $7,674 to $8,957. Both anchors are population averages across all 340B drugs and are not pembrolizumab-specific.

A third public data point is available, flagged as illustrative rather than verified. In October 2025, public Senate HELP Committee testimony placed the illustrative 340B acquisition cost for pembrolizumab at approximately $6,000 per infusion against ASP-based Medicare reimbursement of approximately $12,000 per infusion. The Senate HELP figure is an illustrative public estimate, not a verified ceiling price.

The per-infusion drug spread retained by the 340B hospital outpatient site, using the OIG and CMS anchors, is approximately $3,294 to $4,576.

Same drug. Same dose. Same dispensing pharmacy upstream. Different acquisition cost on the hospital side. Same reimbursement on the Medicare side.

---

Stack the service fee and the drug spread together at the same POS 22 infusion.

The hospital outpatient department collects a $216.49 OPPS service fee. It collects a $12,250.20 ASP+6% drug payment. It acquired the 200 milligram dose at approximately $7,674 to $8,957. The per-infusion drug spread is approximately $3,294 to $4,576 before the administration fee.

At the independent physician office at POS 11, for the same patient, the same drug, the same dose, and the same physician, the MPFS service fee is $57.90. The ASP+6% drug payment is the same $12,250.20. The drug acquisition is at approximately ASP. The per-infusion drug margin before the administration fee is the 6 percent add-on, approximately $693.

Same clinical transaction. Two different revenue outcomes.

---

The only variable that moved between a POS 11 claim and a POS 22 claim for the same patient, the same drug, the same dose, and the same physician is a two-digit field on the claim form.

The field is entered on the CMS-1500 or the UB-04.

The outcome of the field was fixed upstream. It was fixed at the CMS-855A enrollment. It was fixed at the 42 CFR 413.65(d) compliance file. It was fixed before any specific patient was scheduled for infusion.

By the time the patient arrived at the chair, the two-digit field had already been decided.

The classification is upstream. The care is downstream.
