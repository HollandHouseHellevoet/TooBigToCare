# Chapter 3. The Second Look

Chapter 1 was one case.

Chapter 2 was three arguments that do not hold.

Chapter 3 is the test of whether what happened on the Charlotte gallbladder also happens on every other surgery, every office visit, every imaging study, every outpatient service Medicare pays for.

The rate year is 2026. Medicare figures come from CMS CY2026 January OPPS Addendum B, published December 29, 2025. Ambulatory surgical center rates come from CY2026 January ASC Addendum AA, published February 5, 2026. Physician fee schedule rates come from CY2026 MPFS PFREV26B, updated March 10, 2026. All national and unadjusted. Commercial figures are drawn from PayerPrice.com 2025-2026 data and from the Dutch Rojas operator record.

The test is one service category at a time.

---

Start with the same procedure Chapter 1 covered.

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy, CPT 47562, under CY2026 Medicare rates pays $6,176.47 at a hospital outpatient department under OPPS Ambulatory Payment Classification 5361. It pays $3,030.97 at an ambulatory surgical center.

The Medicare facility-fee differential is 2.04 to 1.

That ratio is before commercial uplift. The Charlotte commercial rates from Chapter 1, $34,700 at Atrium Health's hospital outpatient department and $6,800 at the physician-owned surgical facility, extend the gap beyond what Medicare alone produces. The commercial layer pulls the hospital outpatient department higher and holds the ambulatory surgical center lower. The Medicare gap is the floor. The commercial gap is what sits on top of the floor.

The floor is 2.04 to 1. The Charlotte commercial observation is 8.3 to 1.

Move to the next procedure.

---

Cataract extraction with intraocular lens insertion, CPT 66984, is among the most common surgeries performed on the Medicare population.

Under CY2026 Medicare rates, CPT 66984 pays $2,357.81 at a hospital outpatient department under OPPS APC 5491. It pays $1,255.73 at an ambulatory surgical center.

The Medicare facility-fee differential is 1.88 to 1.

On the commercial side, PayerPrice.com 2025-2026 data places the average commercial rate for CPT 66984 at approximately $856 per procedure. The payer-level breakdown:

UnitedHealthcare: $775.69.

Cigna: $805.95.

Aetna: $856.16.

Blue Cross Blue Shield: $987.58.

Medicare, on the same source's basis, sits at approximately $521.

The cataract pattern matches the cholecystectomy pattern. Different procedure. Different anatomy. Same direction.

---

Colonoscopy.

Diagnostic colonoscopy, CPT 45378, under CY2026 Medicare rates pays $950.10 at a hospital outpatient department under OPPS APC 5311. It pays $510.49 at an ambulatory surgical center.

The Medicare facility-fee differential is $439.61 per procedure. The ratio is 1.86 to 1.

Source: CMS OPPS/ASC 2025 final rule.

Gastrointestinal endoscopy. A different organ system. A different specialty. The same direction. A similar ratio to cholecystectomy and cataract.

---

Knee arthroscopy with meniscectomy, CPT 29881.

Under CY2026 Medicare rates, CPT 29881 pays $3,342.87 at a hospital outpatient department under OPPS APC 5113. It pays $1,644.87 at an ambulatory surgical center.

The Medicare facility-fee differential is 2.03 to 1.

On the commercial side, PayerPrice.com places the average commercial rate at approximately $821. UnitedHealthcare $747.50. Cigna $766.34. Aetna $838.00. Blue Cross Blue Shield $933.78. Medicare approximately $654.

Orthopedics. A different specialty again. The same direction.

Four surgical procedures. Four specialties. Four Medicare ratios between 1.86 and 2.04. None of them explainable by acuity. None of them explainable by EMTALA. None of them explainable by uncompensated care. All of them in the same direction.

---

Leave the operating room.

The payment gap is not unique to surgery. It extends to services delivered in an office chair.

An established moderate-complexity office visit, CPT 99214, is the most commonly billed physician service in American medicine. A patient walks in. The physician performs evaluation and management work. The patient walks out. Twenty to thirty minutes, on average.

Under CY2026 Medicare rates, the total Medicare payment for CPT 99214 is $220.52 in a hospital outpatient setting. That figure is the sum of two components: a $136.02 facility fee billed under HCPCS code G0463, the hospital outpatient clinic visit code, paid under OPPS APC 5012, and an $84.50 reduced MPFS professional fee.

The same visit in a freestanding physician office pays $135.61, the MPFS non-facility global rate.

The total-payment differential is 1.63 to 1.

On the commercial side, PayerPrice.com places the average commercial rate for CPT 99214 at approximately $137. UnitedHealthcare $130.37. Cigna $124.63. Aetna $119.38. Blue Cross Blue Shield $174.28. Medicare approximately $113.

An office visit. No operating room. No anesthetic. No surgical equipment. Same visit, different building, different price.

---

Diagnostic imaging.

A complete transthoracic echocardiogram, CPT 93306, is a standard noninvasive cardiac study. Under CY2026 Medicare rates, CPT 93306 pays $625.72 in a hospital outpatient setting. That figure is the sum of a $558.25 technical component paid under OPPS APC 5524 and a $67.47 professional component paid under the MPFS with modifier 26.

The same echocardiogram in a freestanding cardiology office pays $196.73, the MPFS non-facility global.

The total-payment differential is 3.18 to 1.

CPT 93306 is not on the CY2026 ASC covered procedure list. Ambulatory surgical centers cannot bill the code. The comparison is between the hospital outpatient setting and the freestanding physician office, without an ASC path.

The 3.18 to 1 ratio is the largest in the series.

Commercial, from PayerPrice.com: average approximately $302. UnitedHealthcare $247.43. Cigna $297.79. Aetna $303.82. Blue Cross Blue Shield $359.75. Medicare approximately $194.

A sonogram of the heart. A gel. A probe. A technician. Images on a screen. A cardiologist's read. Same procedure. Different building. More than three times the price.

---

One more data point.

Physical therapy is a test case.

Physical therapy codes carry OPPS status indicator A. Status indicator A is a CMS instruction to pay the service under the MPFS regardless of whether it is delivered in a hospital outpatient setting or a freestanding physician office. It removes OPPS from the equation.

A single-visit bundle of a physical therapy evaluation (CPT 97161, low complexity, twenty minutes) plus one unit of therapeutic exercises (CPT 97110, fifteen minutes) pays $126.92 in a hospital outpatient setting.

The same bundle in a freestanding physician office pays $126.92.

Medicare pays identically.

The ratio is one to one.

When Medicare pays identically, the commercial layer behaves differently than when Medicare pays asymmetrically.

In the Dutch Rojas operator record, commercial payer contracts reimburse the same single-visit physical therapy encounter at approximately $310 in a hospital outpatient setting and approximately $95 in an independent physician office.

The commercial differential is 3.26 to 1.

For a service Medicare pays at an identical national rate in both settings.

Commercial payers do not inherit the site-of-service gap from Medicare on every service. On physical therapy, they produce one independent of Medicare. Where Medicare moves, commercial rates generally move with it. Where Medicare does not move, commercial rates can still move on their own.

The site-of-service gap is not only a Medicare phenomenon. It is a commercial phenomenon that exists whether Medicare creates it or not.

---

Seven services across five specialties and two venues.

Cholecystectomy: 2.04 to 1 on Medicare.

Cataract: 1.88 to 1 on Medicare.

Colonoscopy: 1.86 to 1 on Medicare.

Knee arthroscopy: 2.03 to 1 on Medicare.

Office visit: 1.63 to 1 on Medicare total payment.

Echocardiogram: 3.18 to 1 on Medicare total payment.

Physical therapy: 1.00 to 1 on Medicare. 3.26 to 1 on commercial.

The differential is directional. It points in the same direction across every major service category Medicare pays for. It points the same direction whether the service is surgical, evaluative, or diagnostic. It points the same direction in the office as in the operating room. It persists in commercial rates even where Medicare itself is flat.

The differential is not noise. Noise produces ratios that scatter. These ratios do not scatter. They sit between 1.6 and 3.2 on Medicare. They are larger on commercial. They point the same way every time.

The differential is structural.

---

Which raises the question Chapter 4 begins to answer.

Medicare does not pay a single rate for a clinical service. It pays a hospital outpatient department rate. It pays an ambulatory surgical center rate. It pays a physician fee schedule rate. It pays different total-payment figures for the same service depending on which of those rates applies. And which rate applies depends on the building.

The building is not a piece of clinical information. The building is a piece of billing information.

Somewhere between the patient's arrival and the claim's adjudication, a classification is applied. The classification decides what Medicare pays.

The classification exists because Medicare is not one payment system.
