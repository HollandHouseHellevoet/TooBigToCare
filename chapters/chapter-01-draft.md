# Chapter 1. The Same Procedure

A patient in Charlotte, North Carolina is scheduled for a gallbladder removal.

The scheduling is routine. Consent forms, pre-operative instructions, a time and a place to arrive.

The clinical term is laparoscopic cholecystectomy. The billing code is CPT 47562. Four small ports. A camera. The gallbladder is removed intact. The operative window is approximately forty-five minutes.

The patient does not select the facility.

The facility is determined by referral pattern, by insurance network, by proximity, by which operating room is available that week. The patient is told where to arrive. The patient arrives.

Three facilities in the Charlotte market can perform this procedure.

The first is an ambulatory surgical center. Accredited. Staffed. Equipped with the same laparoscopic tower a hospital has.

The second is a physician-owned surgical facility. Accredited. Staffed. Equipped.

The third is a hospital outpatient department operated by Atrium Health. Accredited. Staffed. Equipped.

The physician who performs the operation is the same across all three.

The patient is the same.

The operative window is the same forty-five minutes.

The technique is the same. The ports are placed in the same anatomical positions. The gallbladder is extracted through the same umbilical incision. The post-operative instructions are the same.

The payment is not the same.

---

At the ambulatory surgical center, Medicare's locality-adjusted payment for the procedure is approximately $4,200. The figure is observed directly in operator records from the Charlotte market. The CY2026 national unadjusted ASC rate for CPT 47562, published by CMS in Addendum AA, is $3,030.97. The locality adjustment to Charlotte lifts the figure into the $4,200 range.

That is the first number.

At the physician-owned surgical facility in the same market, the same procedure is paid approximately $6,800.

That is the second number.

At Atrium Health's hospital outpatient department, the negotiated commercial rate for the same procedure is approximately $34,700.

That is the third number.

$4,200.

$6,800.

$34,700.

Same patient. Same physician. Same forty-five minutes. Same four ports. Same camera. Same gallbladder.

Three buildings. Three numbers.

---

The ratio between the highest and the lowest is approximately 8.3 to 1.

Eight point three to one is not a rounding artifact. It is not a statistical outlier. It is not a function of one unusual patient or one unusual contract.

It is the observed rate for moving the same operation, performed by the same physician in the same forty-five minutes, across the Charlotte market.

The variable the patient controls is the operation. The variable someone else controls is the building.

---

The patient receives a bill downstream.

The bill is generated from the contracted rate, run through the plan design, returned to the patient as a share of the total. The patient's share is calibrated against the contracted rate, not against the clinical content. The more expensive the building, the more expensive the share.

The patient does not see the three numbers side by side.

The patient sees one number. The one attached to the building they were routed to.

The $4,200 version of the procedure and the $34,700 version of the procedure are not presented to the patient as a choice.

They are not presented at all.

The invoice arrives with a single facility line and a single balance due. The invoice is the only view of the transaction the patient is given. The $30,500 gap between the lowest and highest facility payments for the same forty-five-minute operation is not on the invoice. It is not anywhere the patient can see it.

---

The surgeon did not set the numbers.

The surgeon performed the procedure. The surgeon billed for the professional component. The surgeon's take-home pay does not vary by a factor of 8.3 across the three buildings.

The building sets the numbers.

More precisely: the classification attached to the building sets the numbers. Ambulatory surgical center. Physician-owned surgical facility. Hospital outpatient department. Three classifications, three fee schedules, three rates for the same clinical act.

The classification was not chosen on the day of surgery. The classification was established upstream, by statute, by regulation, by enrollment paperwork filed months or years before the patient was scheduled.

By the time the patient arrives at the building, the number is already set.

---

There is no clinical justification inside these three numbers.

There is no case-mix explanation inside these three numbers. There is no acuity gradient, no additional service rendered at the $34,700 site that is absent at the $4,200 site. The four ports go in the same places. The camera is the same camera. The gallbladder is the same gallbladder.

The $30,500 difference is not a function of the operation.

It is a function of the classification of the room the operation happens in.

---

One procedure. One market. One surgeon. One patient.

$4,200 at the ambulatory surgical center.

$6,800 at the physician-owned surgical facility.

$34,700 at Atrium Health's hospital outpatient department.

Eight point three to one.
