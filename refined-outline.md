# The $275 Billion Heist. Refined Outline

Scannable artifact. One proof sentence per chapter. Tagged against the thesis. Physician, employer, and patient placements noted inline. Built from `outline-pressure-test.md` with the 8 pressure-test fixes applied. Date: 2026-04-10.

## The thesis (four parts)

1. Positioning over performance.
2. Written into law.
3. Protected by the lobbying apparatus of the beneficiaries.
4. Invisible to the physicians, employers, and patients who fund it.

Every proof sentence below lands at least one of these four claims.

## How to read this outline

Each chapter is one line. Each line states the length, the thesis tags, and a single proof sentence (the claim the reader holds after reading, not the topic the chapter covers). Pre-plant notes are attached inline to Ch 1, Ch 9, and Ch 17 because the physician, the employer, and the patient have to be present before their spotlight chapters. Non-advancing chapters are flagged in their own section at the bottom. The verification checklist at the end is the check the reader runs against the file in a single pass.

## Legend

- `[POS]` positioning over performance
- `[LAW]` written into law
- `[LOB]` protected by lobbying
- `[INV]` invisible to funders

A chapter may carry more than one tag. A chapter with no tag fails the thesis check and is flagged.

## Physician, employer, patient placement

**Physician.** Voice from Ch 1. Subject at Ch 19. Two load-bearing moments. Dutch is the narrative lens from the opening sentence of the book, so the physician is present by construction from page one. Ch 19 is the chapter where the physician stops narrating and becomes the subject. No additional pre-plant required.

**Employer.** Spotlight at Ch 21. Two required pre-plants. First in Ch 9, naming employers as the largest single funder of the $275B figure, with at least one named self-funded employer attached so the number arrives owned by someone. Second in Ch 17, noting that employers are conspicuously absent from the CMS Healthcare Advisory Committee rooms where hospital lobbyists sit. Absence is part of the lobbying proof. Without these pre-plants the reader reaches Ch 9 without knowing whose $275 billion it is, which is a thesis failure rather than a style failure.

**Patient.** Spotlight at Ch 22. Two required pre-plants. First in Ch 1, naming the anomaly from the patient's invoice rather than from the hospital chargemaster, so the patient is felt from page one. Second in Ch 9, locating the patient share of the $275B via premiums, deductibles, and cost-sharing, parallel to the employer pre-plant.

Reasoning. The thesis names physicians, employers, and patients as the funders for whom the system is invisible. A book that first names employers at Ch 21 and patients at Ch 22 reaches Ch 9 (the $275B reveal) with no named owner on the money. The fix is not to move the spotlight chapters earlier (that collapses architecture into advocacy before the reader has felt the mechanism). The fix is three pre-plant moments, visible on the scaffold above, recorded here in full.

## Verification checklist

Read the file top to bottom in one pass. Three questions, in order.

1. Does every chapter line carry exactly one proof sentence, stated as a claim the reader will believe after reading rather than a topic the chapter covers? If any sentence uses "discusses," "introduces," "covers," or "explores," rewrite it as a claim.
2. Does every proof carry at least one of the four thesis tags `[POS]` `[LAW]` `[LOB]` `[INV]`? If any chapter has no tag, flag it in the non-advancing chapter check section above.
3. Does the physician appear at Ch 1 (voice) and Ch 19 (subject)? Does the employer appear at Ch 9 (pre-plant), Ch 17 (pre-plant), and Ch 21 (spotlight)? Does the patient appear at Ch 1 (pre-plant), Ch 9 (pre-plant), and Ch 22 (spotlight)? At each of those chapters the figure has to be present. At no other chapter is the figure forced in.

If all three questions return yes the artifact is locked. If any question returns no, the specific failure points to the specific fix. Question 1 failures rewrite sentences. Question 2 failures flag chapters. Question 3 failures adjust the pre-plant notes on Ch 1, Ch 9, and Ch 17.
